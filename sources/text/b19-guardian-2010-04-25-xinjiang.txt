China has removed the hardline Communist party leader of Xinjiang, less than a year after ethnic riots in the restive north-western province left almost 200 dead.

Wang Lequan's new position as deputy secretary of the party's political and legislative affairs committee was seen by many as a demotion, although his 15-year tenure as the region's top official was highly unusual.

The city's party secretary and the regional police chief were replaced days later. Analysts suggested Beijing might also replace Wang, but would be reluctant to do so immediately in case it looked like a sign of weakness.
